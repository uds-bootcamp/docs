# Recommendations for Mentors

## **Introduction**

As a mentor, your role is central to the success of the participants and the integrity of the UDS Bootcamp. This guide synthesizes the key principles from the program's governing documents—the Code of Conduct, Rules of Participation, and Capstone Recommendations—to provide a clear framework for your responsibilities. Your primary functions are to provide expert technical guidance, model professional behavior, and uphold the academic standards of the program.

---

## 1. Core Principles of Mentorship

Your conduct sets the tone for the entire cohort. It is essential to embody the program's core principles in all interactions.

* **Model Professionalism and Respect:** Your behavior should serve as an example of the professional conduct expected in the workplace. Treat all participants with respect, regardless of their experience level, background, or identity.
* **Foster a Safe and Inclusive Environment:** Actively contribute to a learning environment where all individuals feel welcome, valued, and safe from harassment or discrimination. Be familiar with the list of unacceptable behaviors, such as offensive comments, intimidation, and unwelcome advances.
* **Promote Collaboration and Academic Integrity:** Encourage participants to help one another as part of a collective learning effort. However, ensure all submitted work remains original unless collaboration is explicitly permitted. Guide participants to solutions rather than providing direct answers to uphold academic integrity.
* **Maintain Confidentiality:** Handle all reports of Code of Conduct violations with discretion. Do not share private communications or personal information about participants without their explicit consent.

## 2. Guidance on Technical Work and Assignments

While assisting with assignments and labs, it is crucial to reinforce the program's structure and expectations for quality.

* **Uphold Submission Deadlines:** Be aware that all assignments have firm deadlines. Remind your mentees that a 24-hour grace period may only be granted if requested *before* the original deadline. Do not grant extensions yourself; direct participants to the instructor.
* **Ensure Good-Faith Effort:** Participants are required to make a good-faith effort on all submissions. If you observe that a participant's work is substantially incomplete or demonstrates a clear lack of effort, it may be classified as a non-submission. Flag this pattern to the program administrator.

## 3. Mentoring the Capstone Project

The capstone project is the culminating experience of the bootcamp. Your guidance is critical in ensuring participants produce a portfolio-worthy piece of work that meets program standards.

* **Reinforce Foundational Principles:** Ensure your mentees' projects align with the core requirements:
    * The project must demonstrate the core competencies taught in the bootcamp.
    * It should solve a non-trivial problem, avoiding generic clones of simple applications.
    * The project must be data-centric, built around creating, manipulating, or presenting data.
* **Enforce a Manageable Scope:** Guide participants in defining a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that is achievable within the program's timeframe. Help them categorize ambitious ideas as "stretch goals" to demonstrate prioritization skills.
* **Follow the Systematic Framework:** Steer your mentees through the prescribed project execution framework:
    1.  **Ideation & Feasibility:** Help them validate their ideas against technical alignment, data availability, and time scope.
    2.  **Scoping & Planning:** Review their formal project proposal and ensure they break the MVP into small, actionable tasks using a project management tool.
    3.  **Execution & Presentation:** Stress the importance of frequent Git commits, maintaining a comprehensive `README.md`, and deploying the application to a live server.

## 4. Upholding Program Rules and Engagement

You are a key partner in maintaining a productive and fair learning environment for all participants.

* **Understand the Three-Strike Policy:** Be thoroughly familiar with the actions that result in a "strike". These include:
    * An unexcused absence.
    * Failure to submit a major assignment without prior communication.
    * Repeated non-participation (e.g., camera off without permission, lack of response).
    * A participant will be removed upon accruing three strikes.
* **Promote Active Participation:** Encourage your mentees to keep their cameras on during sessions, as this is a requirement to foster community and accountability. Remind them that active engagement in discussions and group activities is expected.
* **Absence and Punctuality:** Reinforce that attendance at all live sessions is mandatory. If a mentee is absent, remind them they are solely responsible for catching up on missed material. Note that a maximum of two unexcused absences is permitted.

## 5. Communication and Reporting Protocol

Clear communication and timely escalation are essential for addressing issues before they become critical.

* **Adhere to Communication Guidelines:** Model best practices in all communications.
    * Use public channels for questions that may benefit others and direct messages for private matters.
    * Mute your microphone when not speaking in video calls and use the "raise hand" feature.
    * Ensure your own virtual background is professional and free of disruptive content.
* **Escalate Issues to the Program Administrator:** Your role is to mentor, not to enforce all disciplinary action. You must report the following to the program administrator via private message:
    * Any witnessed or reported violation of the Code of Conduct.
    * A participant who is at risk of accruing a strike or has already accrued one.
    * Suspected cases of plagiarism or academic dishonesty.
    * A participant expressing their intent to withdraw from the program.
    * Any concerns regarding a participant's well-being or a significant personal emergency impacting their participation.
