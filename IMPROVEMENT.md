# Bootcamp Improvement by Participants

This document describes how participants can propose, champion, and verify improvements to the UDS Bootcamp. It complements the existing governance docs by providing a clear pathway for participant-led enhancements to curriculum, operations, and community practices.

## Guiding Principles

- **Constructive Collaboration:** Improvements should aim to support instructors, mentors, and peers rather than shift responsibility onto others.
- **Data-Informed Suggestions:** Recommendations require evidence (survey data, attendance metrics, qualitative feedback) so the leadership team can assess impact.
- **Inclusive Representation:** Voices from multiple cohorts, time zones, and backgrounds are encouraged to minimize bias in proposed changes.
- **Transparency and Follow-Through:** Participants who propose changes commit to participating in the implementation or evaluation phases when possible.

## Improvement Lifecycle

1. **Identify the Opportunity**
   - Document the current state and the gap or friction.
   - Gather informal feedback from peers to confirm the issue is shared.
2. **Draft a Proposal**
   - Summarize the problem, desired outcome, and measurable success criteria.
   - Note dependencies (tools, mentor support, scheduling).
3. **Submit Feedback**
   - Use the official feedback form or ticketing channel listed in the onboarding pack.
   - Include supporting evidence (screenshots, metrics, participant quotes) when available.
4. **Leadership Review**
   - Program administrators acknowledge receipt within two business days.
   - Complex proposals may require a live discussion during office hours.
5. **Pilot and Iterate**
   - Approved ideas run as pilots with clear start/end dates.
   - Collect feedback during the pilot to decide on adoption, revision, or rollback.
6. **Document and Share**
   - Publish outcomes and lessons learned in the cohort knowledge base or retro notes.

## Participant Working Groups

- **Formation:** Any participant can assemble a working group of 3–6 peers with diverse skill sets relevant to the improvement area.
- **Scope:** Groups focus on time-bound initiatives (e.g., revamping onboarding, refining capstone feedback rubrics).
- **Mentor Support:** Request a mentor liaison via the program administrator to ensure alignment with academic standards.
- **Deliverables:** Each group produces a brief report with problem statement, actions taken, results, and recommendations for future cohorts.

## Feedback Channels

- **Weekly Retrospectives:** Dedicated time to surface quick fixes and gather anonymous input.
- **Surveys:** Run by the program team at mid-point and graduation; participants may request additional pulse surveys for targeted topics.
- **Open Forum Sessions:** Monthly meetings where participants present proposals to leadership and mentors.
- **Async Threads:** Use the designated Slack/Discord channels for ongoing discussion and documentation.

## Tracking and Accountability

- **Improvement Log:** Program administrators maintain a shared log of proposals, owners, milestones, and status (submitted, in review, pilot, shipped).
- **Metrics Review:** Each improvement should define success metrics (e.g., reduced assignment confusion, higher attendance, faster code review turnaround) and evaluate them post-implementation.
- **Recognition:** Participants who lead impactful improvements receive acknowledgment during graduation and may be invited to mentor future cohorts.

## Conflict Resolution

- **Escalation Path:** If a proposal stalls or feedback is ignored, escalate to the Program Administrator, then to the Head of Bootcamp if needed.
- **Respectful Disagreement:** Maintain professionalism when debating changes; focus on data and outcomes, not individuals.
- **Final Decisions:** Leadership holds final authority to approve or decline proposals, with an explanation provided for transparency.

## Continuous Improvement Culture

- Incorporate improvement discussions into cohort standups or project check-ins.
- Encourage new cohorts to review the improvement log so successful practices persist.
- Celebrate incremental progress; not every change needs to be transformative to be valuable.
